## ApxAvroBill

### Purpose of this appendix

This appendix sets out the detail of the Avro final bill / handover bill and explains why the transferred balance was not properly supported by the figures shown on the bill.

The central issue is simple: the bill claimed a debit balance of **£1,124.93**, but the figures shown inside the bill do not reconcile to that amount.

Using the signs shown on the bill, the logical conclusion is that the account was in **customer credit by £950.45**.

If Octopus disputes that conclusion, it must explain the sign convention used and provide a proper reconciliation. It has not done so.

### Background

The Avro bill handed over to Octopus was one of the starting points for the ongoing account problems.

Octopus appears to have accepted the transferred Avro balance without properly checking whether the bill itself made sense.

At the time of the transfer I was not closely tracking Avro or Octopus billing. I had not expected the supplier failure and did not immediately understand the implications of the handover. It was only later, when my Octopus account appeared to show substantial debt, that I reviewed the Avro bill and noticed that something was wrong.

I raised my concern with Octopus by email on **3 November 2022**.

### The Avro bill

The Avro bill was dated **29 September 2021** and covered the period **28 August 2021 to 26 September 2021**.

The bill claimed an overall **DR** balance of:

    £1,124.93

That means Avro, and later Octopus, treated the bill as showing that I owed **£1,124.93**.

However, the figures within the bill do not support that conclusion.

### Charges shown on the bill

The bill showed gas and electricity charges of approximately **£87.24**.

The internal split was:

    £53.27   Electricity
    £33.97   Gas
    ------
    £87.24   Total

There may be a minor one-penny rounding or presentation difference elsewhere on the bill, but this does not affect the main issue.

### Balance figures shown on the bill

The key figures shown on the bill were:

    -768.66   Previous balance
      87.24   New charges
    -269.03   Payments received

The signs are central.

The bill shows the previous balance and the payment received as negative figures. It shows the new charges as a positive figure.

### The stated Avro bill total

Avro stated the final balance as:

    DR £1,124.93

However, that figure is not produced by adding the figures using the signs shown on the bill.

Using the signs shown:

    -768.66
      87.24
    -269.03
    -------
    -950.45

That produces **-£950.45**, not **DR £1,124.93**.

On the bill's own apparent accounting convention, that is a customer credit.

### How £1,124.93 appears to have been produced

The stated figure of **£1,124.93** can be reproduced only by adding the absolute values of the figures:

     768.66
      87.24
     269.03
    -------
    1124.93

That calculation ignores the signs on the bill.

That is not a coherent financial calculation. A customer payment and a supplier charge cannot both increase the customer's debt. Whatever sign convention is used, those items must have opposite accounting effects.

### Correct reading of the bill

The bill appears to use the following convention:

* supplier charges are shown as positive figures;
* customer payments are shown as negative figures;
* a negative balance represents a customer credit.

Following that convention, the Avro bill shows:

    Opening credit balance               768.66
    Payment received                     269.03
    Less new charges                     -87.24
    -------------------------------------------
    Customer credit                      950.45

That is the logical reading of the bill.

The account was not in debit by **£1,124.93**. On the figures shown, it was in credit by **£950.45**.

If Octopus says otherwise, it needs to provide evidence and a reconciliation showing why the signs on the bill should not be followed.

### Octopus' later correction

Octopus later stated that a colleague had corrected the Avro balance by applying a credit of **£831.49**, reducing the transferred balance from **£1,124.93** to **£293.44**.

That is important.

It shows that Octopus accepted the transferred Avro balance was wrong. However, Octopus has never explained why it corrected the balance only partly.

Octopus has not explained:

* how the **£831.49** correction was calculated;
* why the remaining **£293.44** was still treated as valid debt;
* what underlying Avro data was checked;
* whether the signs on the Avro bill were considered;
* whether the bill was reconciled against actual meter readings, payments and charges.

In effect, Octopus moved from one unsupported figure to another unsupported figure.

### My position

My position is that the Avro bill should be read according to the figures and signs it actually contains.

On that basis, the bill shows a customer credit of **£950.45**.

Octopus was apparently willing to rely on the Avro bill when it treated the account as owing **£1,124.93**. Once the same bill is read correctly, Octopus cannot simply disregard the result because it favours the customer.

If Octopus has reliable evidence showing a different position, it should provide it. In the absence of that evidence, the logical conclusion from the bill is that the Avro account was in credit.

On the face of the bill:

* the stated debit balance of **£1,124.93** is produced only by ignoring the signs;
* the internal figures, read consistently, produce a customer credit of **£950.45**;
* Octopus later applied a substantial corrective credit, confirming that the transferred balance was wrong;
* Octopus continued to treat a residual **£293.44** as debt without explaining why.

In those circumstances, the Avro-derived debit balance should not stand.

### Conclusion

The Avro bill does not support the transferred debit balance applied to my Octopus account.

The calculation shown on the bill supports the opposite conclusion: a customer credit of **£950.45**.

Unless Octopus can provide a proper reconciliation showing otherwise, that is the balance which should be applied.

Octopus should therefore provide the underlying reconciliation showing:

* the original Avro opening balance;
* all charges;
* all payments;
* the sign convention used;
* how the **£1,124.93** debit was produced;
* how the **£831.49** correction was calculated;
* why **£293.44** remained payable.

Without that reconciliation, I do not believe the Avro-derived balance can fairly or lawfully be relied upon.
