PART 1 — BACKGROUND
========================

When Octopus took over my account from Avro, I was presented with a final Avro bill (Ref: [###]) which did not make sense on its face.

The bill showed a headline balance of approximately £1,124.93 “DR”, which Octopus treated as a debt owed by me.

However, the detailed figures on the bill were as follows:

- (-) £789.00 — Balance brought forward  
- (+) £87.XX — New energy charges  
- (-) £269.03 — Payment made by me  

There was also an additional line item described as an “admin fee” of (-) £269.03, the meaning of which has never been explained.

Interpreting the figures using their natural signs:

- Charges (+) increase customer liability  
- Payments / credits (-) reduce customer liability  

The net position from the figures shown is approximately:

(-789.00 + 87.XX - 269.03) ≈ -£950.65

This indicates a substantial credit balance, not a debt.

It later became apparent that the £1,124.93 figure corresponds to the sum of the absolute values of the figures, ignoring their signs.

This is not a valid accounting approach. A payment cannot be added to a charge to produce a liability figure. Doing so reverses the meaning of the transaction and produces a misleading result.

Despite this, Octopus treated the account as being in debt.

At no point was a clear, formal explanation provided as to how this figure was derived or why it was considered valid.

---

Following this, Octopus applied an unexplained credit (approximately £8XX) to the account, reducing the assumed debt but still leaving a residual balance of approximately £2XX.

This residual amount was then added to my Octopus account without consultation or explanation.

This sequence of actions demonstrates:

- Acceptance that the original Avro figure was unreliable (by applying a large correction)
- Continued reliance on that same figure to assert a remaining debt
- Lack of transparency in how any corrected balance was calculated

---

During this period:

- I raised concerns with Octopus and requested clarification
- Octopus staff requested information from me about Avro, suggesting they did not have full records themselves
- No coherent explanation was provided
- No apology was offered for the confusion or time required to investigate the issue

Octopus appeared to prioritise meter readings and payment collection over resolving the underlying billing inconsistency.
