## Formal Complaint 1 — Direct Debit Taken Despite Explicit Refusal

This complaint concerns Octopus taking a substantially increased Direct Debit payment after I had expressly refused authority for that payment.

The email trail is provided in the appendix. I summarise the key complaint points below.

### 1. Octopus took money after explicit refusal

On 19 May 2023, I replied to Octopus’s proposed Direct Debit increase and stated clearly:

> “SORRY BUT I AM NOT AUTHORISING THIS AT PRESENT.”

I also said:

> “Any attempt to take that amount of money would be met with a direct-debit recall.”

Despite that, Octopus later took over £500 from my bank account. I then raised a formal complaint stating that the money had been taken despite my explicit instruction not to do so. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

This is the core issue. Octopus may have had the right to review my Direct Debit. That is not the same as a right to take a substantially increased payment after I had expressly refused authority.

### 2. Octopus appeared to rely on Ofgem as justification for overriding my instruction

Octopus’s response was especially concerning because it appeared to say that my direct instruction did not matter because Octopus had regulatory obligations.

Octopus wrote:

> “regardless of your ‘direct instruction’ we have regulations we must follow or face fines as a company.”

They then said that as long as they notified me within five days, they could amend payments to follow Ofgem guidelines. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

That is a serious position for an energy supplier to take. The apparent implication is that Octopus considered itself entitled to remove money from my bank account against my express refusal because of Ofgem rules or guidance.

I ask the Ombudsman to require Octopus to identify the precise rule, regulation, licence condition, or Ofgem guidance on which it relies for that proposition.

If Octopus cannot identify such a rule, then its reliance on Ofgem was misleading and unfair. It gave the impression that I was not entitled to object, and that Octopus had no choice but to take the money.

### 3. Direct Debit review is not the same as payment authority

Octopus repeatedly referred to Direct Debit review obligations, account balance, estimated usage, and missing gas readings.

Those issues may explain why Octopus wanted to review the Direct Debit.

They do not answer the actual complaint.

The complaint was not: “Can Octopus review a Direct Debit?”

The complaint was: “Can Octopus take a disputed increased Direct Debit after the customer has expressly refused authority?”

Octopus never properly answered that question.

### 4. Octopus misframed the complaint

After I raised the complaint, Octopus repeatedly reframed it as if I was merely unhappy about an automatic Direct Debit review.

Tina-Marina described the matter as:

> “you were not happy about the automatic direct debit review we carried out.”

That was not the complaint. I had stated that Octopus had acted against my direct instruction and taken money without my permission. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

This misframing matters because it allowed Octopus to answer a different complaint from the one I had actually made.

### 5. Octopus used terms and conditions as a shield, without answering the authority point

Octopus referred to its terms and conditions and said I had agreed to them.

Even if Octopus had contractual terms allowing it to review Direct Debit levels, Octopus still needed to explain how those terms authorised taking a disputed increased payment after I had expressly refused it.

It did not do so.

A general term about reviewing Direct Debits cannot simply be treated as a blank cheque over a customer’s bank account.

### 6. Octopus pressured me with an “accept DD or pay within 14 days” position

Octopus also wrote:

> “If you do not agree with our terms and conditions or the OFGEM rules, then I recommend you cancel your direct debit and pay for the bills as and when you get them. These must be cleared within 14 days of receiving them to prevent the debt process.” :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

That was worrying and unreasonable in context.

I was not refusing to pay properly owed charges. I was asking Octopus to explain the account, provide documents, justify the sudden increase, and not take a large disputed payment while that was unresolved.

Instead, Octopus effectively placed me under pressure: accept its Direct Debit position, or face accelerated payment expectations and the debt process.

### 7. Octopus shifted blame while failing to provide the information needed

Octopus repeatedly suggested that I had “failed” to provide a preferred Direct Debit amount or gas meter reading.

That was unfairly simplistic.

I had asked for the documents and explanation needed to understand the account position. This included bills, opening balance information, readings, estimates, and the basis of the forecast.

Rather than pausing and resolving that information gap, Octopus pressed ahead with the increased Direct Debit and then blamed me for not accepting or proposing an alternative.

### 8. Formal complaint handling failure

Octopus logged the complaint under reference 549175. However, the correspondence does not show a proper formal written response to the complaint as actually stated.

The key complaint was never properly answered:

> What authority did Octopus rely on to take the increased Direct Debit after I expressly refused authorisation?

Nor does the thread show a clear final response or deadlock letter.

This is a separate complaint-handling failure. Octopus did not simply make a disputed payment decision. It then failed to engage properly with the formal complaint about that decision.

### 9. Trust breach

Direct Debit depends on trust. Once I had expressly told Octopus not to take the increased amount, it should not have proceeded without resolving the dispute.

By taking the money anyway, then relying on general Direct Debit review wording, Ofgem, terms and conditions, account balance and missing readings, Octopus destroyed my confidence that it would respect the limits of the Direct Debit arrangement.

### 10. Impact

This caused:

- financial disruption;
- worry about further large withdrawals;
- loss of trust in Direct Debit as a payment mechanism;
- stress and inconvenience;
- time spent reconstructing the account history;
- time spent trying to get Octopus to answer the complaint it had actually been sent.

### Redress requested

I ask the Ombudsman to require Octopus to:

1. acknowledge that it should not have taken the increased Direct Debit after my explicit refusal;
2. identify the exact rule or contractual term it says allowed it to do so;
3. explain why it relied on Ofgem in the way it did;
4. confirm whether it accepts that Direct Debit review powers are distinct from authority to take a disputed increased payment;
5. compensate me for distress, inconvenience, financial disruption, and time spent dealing with this issue;
6. compensate me separately for poor complaint handling;
7. confirm what steps it has taken to prevent similar conduct happening again.
