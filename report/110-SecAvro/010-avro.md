## SecAvro

### Summary

Octopus took over my energy account around September 2021, following the failure of Avro Energy. The first indication I received of Octopus' involvement was through emails relating to Direct Debit setup. I had not been following the collapse of energy suppliers at the time, so I was not expecting this transfer.

Towards the end of 2022 I became increasingly concerned about the level of debt apparently showing on my account.

On checking back, I reviewed the Avro final bill / handover bill and found that the figures did not appear to reconcile. The bill claimed an overall debit balance of **£1,124.93**, but the internal figures shown on the bill did not support that conclusion.

The bill showed:

    -768.66   Previous balance
      87.24   New charges
    -269.03   Payments received
    --------
    -950.45   Net total using the signs shown on the bill

That calculation does not produce a debit balance of **£1,124.93**. It produces a negative figure, which on the bill's own apparent convention indicates a customer credit.

Full detail is set out in **ApxAvroBill**.

### Details

#### Summing the totals and payments

The contents of the Avro bill, if summed using the signs shown on the bill, give the following calculation:

    -768.66   Previous balance
      87.24   New charges
    -269.03   Payments received
    --------
    -950.45   Total using the signs presented

This does not correspond to the stated debit balance of **£1,124.93**.

#### Where does £1,124.93 appear to come from?

The stated debit balance can be reproduced only by adding the absolute values of the figures shown:

     768.66
      87.24
     269.03
    -------
    1124.93

In other words, the stated figure appears to be the result of ignoring the signs attached to the figures.

That is not a valid way to treat credits, charges and customer payments in a financial calculation. A customer payment and a supplier charge cannot logically have the same accounting effect.

#### Bringing the matter to Octopus' attention

I raised this issue with Octopus by email on **3 November 2022**. At that stage I was confused and concerned about the state of my account, particularly because later Octopus bill summaries were showing substantial apparent debt despite my paying the recommended Direct Debit.

A later bill summary for **10 November 2022 to 27 November 2022** showed a debit balance of **£1,614.13**. I understand that this did not include the Avro balance. Had I understood that at the time, my concern would have been even greater.

#### Octopus' handling of the Avro balance

I believe I had a telephone conversation with Octopus support about this issue. I recall not being satisfied with the explanation, but at that stage I did not have the full facts or a clear understanding of the supplier handover process. I therefore expected Octopus to provide a formal written explanation of what had happened and how the balance would be corrected.

I did not receive any clear written explanation of the Avro bill calculation or of Octopus' reconciliation process.

The only written reference I have located is a brief statement in an email dealing with wider matters:

    One of our colleagues, Becks, previously corrected the avro balance by applying a credit of £831.49 this reduced the balance that was transferred to us from -£1,124.93 to -£293.44 ...

This appears to have been the practical resolution applied by Octopus: a credit of **£831.49** was added, reducing the transferred balance from **£1,124.93** to **£293.44**.

However, I have never been given a proper explanation of how the **£831.49** correction was calculated, why the remaining **£293.44** continued to be treated as debt, or what reconciliation was carried out against the underlying Avro bill.

#### The Avro balance reconsidered

If the accounting signs used within the bill are followed consistently, a different interpretation emerges.

The bill appears to use the following convention:

* charges, meaning money owed by the customer to Avro, are shown as positive figures;
* payments, meaning money paid by the customer, are shown as negative figures.

On that basis:

* the opening balance of **-£768.66** appears to indicate a customer credit;
* the new charges of **£87.24** reduce that credit;
* the customer payment of **-£269.03** increases that credit.

That produces the following position:

    Credit balance brought forward       768.66
    Payment received                     269.03
    Less new charges                     -87.24
    -------------------------------------------
    Apparent customer credit             950.45

On the face of the bill, this appears to be a more coherent interpretation than treating the account as being in debit by **£1,124.93**.

I am not saying that this must be the final answer if Octopus can provide reliable contrary records. However, in the absence of a proper reconciliation, the bill itself appears to support a customer credit rather than a customer debt.

#### Residual Avro balance of £293.44

Octopus later treated a residual Avro balance of **£293.44** as payable.

I do not accept that this amount has been properly justified. If the original Avro bill was internally inconsistent, and if Octopus accepted that a substantial correction of **£831.49** was required, then Octopus needed to explain clearly why any remaining balance was still owed.

That explanation has not been provided.

### Complaint points

1. Octopus accepted and applied an Avro balance which, on the face of the bill, was internally inconsistent.

2. Octopus did not identify or resolve the issue before I raised it, despite having operated the account for over a year by that point.

3. Octopus applied a substantial corrective credit of **£831.49**, but did not provide a clear calculation or explanation of how that figure was reached.

4. Octopus continued to treat a residual **£293.44** as debt without explaining why that amount remained valid.

5. Octopus did not provide a proper written reconciliation of the Avro bill, the transferred balance, the corrective credit, and the remaining balance.

6. Octopus did not explain what checks or due diligence were carried out before incorporating the Avro balance into my account.

7. Octopus did not engage with the accounting-sign issue apparent on the face of the Avro bill.

8. As a result, I have been left to reconstruct and challenge the position myself, years after the transfer.

### Supplementary questions

* **AVRO.SQ1** - Please provide a detailed breakdown of how the corrective credit of **£831.49** applied to my account was calculated, including the underlying units, rates, billing periods and any adjustments applied.

* **AVRO.SQ2** - Please explain the basis on which the Avro balance continued to be treated as a debt following the application of a substantial corrective credit, including the account balance position before and after the adjustment.

* **AVRO.SQ3** - Please describe the verification and due diligence performed on the Avro balance prior to its application to my account, including what checks were carried out, what data was relied upon, and whether any validation against underlying billing records was performed.

* **AVRO.SQ4** - Please describe the formal mechanism by which the Avro account was transferred to Octopus, including the data sources used, the format of the data received, and whether any reconciliation or transformation was applied before it was incorporated into my account.

* **AVRO.SQ5** - Please explain the basis on which the residual balance of **£293.44** was added to my account in November 2024, including what triggered this entry at that time, what period it relates to, and how the amount was calculated.

### Restitution sought

I ask Octopus to:

* remove and refund any Avro-derived balance which has not been properly justified;

* provide a full recalculation of my running account balance after correcting the Avro position;

* pay interest at the applicable statutory rate on any positive balance which should have been available to me;

* compensate me **£150** for the time and effort required to identify, raise, and now re-address this issue through the complaint process.
