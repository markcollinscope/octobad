## Background

Direct debits, when they work well, are a great convenience to both the customer and the business using them. Minor ammendments can be dealt with without over-heavy process - and a reasonable and well understood degree of flexibility can save time for all parties.

However, the direct debit scheme relies on mutual trust. Trust that a supplier will not overstep the mark in pursuit of own commercial interest, that they will always give sufficient notice of any variance - proportianate to amount of variance in question (it will be easy for some customers - at least - to accept a variance of 10 or 20 pounds with a few days notice - perhaps. It is completely undreasonable to expect a consumer to be able to provide hundreds of pounts in additional funds at short, or even long term notice. 

By short term I'm thinking of around a week's notice. No less.
By long term I'm thinking of around a full calendar months notice.

Each case is of course, unique, however I was surprised - as per the below - that a professional organisation like Octopus did not seem to have my understanding of how these things should work. We live and learn. I have certainly learned, the hard way it seems.
